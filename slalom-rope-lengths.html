Slalom rope lengths

The standard shortline chart: feet off, the rope in meters and in feet, and the color of each loop.

How off works

Off is how many feet a loop sits below a nominal 75-foot line. The long line is that baseline: 23 m, measured at 75 ft 5.5 in, zero feet off. The boat shortens the rope by putting the next loop over the pylon. 15 off, 22 off, and 28 off are the first three shortenings.

The meter column is the length in the rule book. The feet column is that same length from the AWSA tow-line diagram, measured under 20 kg (44 lb) from inside the handle to inside the loop. Skiers still use the round name: 15 off is the 60-foot name (75 minus 15) and measures 59 ft 10.5 in. 22 off is the 53-foot name and measures 52 ft 6 in. 28 off is the 47-foot name and measures 46 ft 9 in.

Questions

What does 15 off mean?

15 off is the first short line: 15 feet off a nominal 75-foot rope, which is why skiers call it a 60-foot line. The red loop is 18.25 m from the handle. Measured under 20 kg (44 lb), that length is 59 ft 10.5 in.

What are 22 off and 28 off in meters?

22 off is 16.00 m, the orange loop, measured at 52 ft 6 in. 28 off is 14.25 m, the yellow loop, measured at 46 ft 9 in.

Why doesn't the length in feet equal 75 minus the off number?

The off number is a name counted down from 75. The rope is built to the meter length and measured from inside the handle to inside the loop. 22 off is the 53-foot name (75 minus 22) and measures 52 ft 6 in. The chart uses the measured length.

What color is each loop?

Red at 15 off, orange at 22, yellow at 28, green at 32, blue at 35, violet (blue/white) at 38, white at 39.5, pink at 41, black at 43, and red again at 44 off. That order is IWWF Rule 5.09e. The long-line end loop need not be color coded. 45 off (9.25 m) is on the AWSA scoring matrix and has no color in the IWWF list. Older charts sometimes call the 10.75 m loop neutral; the 2026 rule names it white.
